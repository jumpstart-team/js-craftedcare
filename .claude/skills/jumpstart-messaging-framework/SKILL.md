# JumpStart Messaging Framework

Distilled from JumpStart DPC Solutions' Hint Launch Bootcamp workshop:
*"Building Your Website: Turning Interest Into Patient Action"*

This framework governs all patient-facing and prospect-facing copy produced
for DPC, concierge, and membership medical practice clients. It is not
optional. Every headline, CTA, caption, and section description must pass
through this lens before delivery.

---

## The Core Premise

**The website is the first appointment.** Before any patient calls or books,
they land on the site and make a fast, quiet judgment. The website has one
job: help the right patient take the next step.

**Confused patients don't schedule. They leave.**

The "next step" varies by context:
- Schedule a meet & greet
- Join the waitlist
- Become a member
- Call the practice
- Request more info
- Attend an info session

Pick ONE per page or campaign. Never give patients a menu of equal options.

---

## The Five Silent Patient Questions

Every patient who lands on a DPC or concierge practice site is silently
asking these five questions, in this order. Copy must answer them in sequence:

1. **"Do I understand what this is?"** — Clarity. Are you immediately legible?
2. **"Does this feel like it's for me?"** — Fit. Do they see themselves?
3. **"Can I trust this clinician?"** — Credibility. Does the doctor feel real?
4. **"Is this worth paying for?"** — Value. Is the cost justified?
5. **"What do I do next?"** — Action. Is the path obvious?

If copy skips any of these, it fails that patient at that point. Answer all
five, in order, on every homepage.

---

## The Patient Conversion Path

Website visit → **Clarity** (they get it, fast) → **Trust** (clinician feels
credible) → **Action** (they book, join, or inquire)

**All copy must serve one of these three stages.** If a sentence doesn't move
a patient toward clarity, trust, or action, cut it or rewrite it until it does.

---

## What Patients Are Actually Buying

Patients do not buy "a healthcare delivery model" or "a membership-based
primary care alternative." They buy:

- Time (longer visits, unhurried)
- Access (reach the doctor when something comes up)
- Trust (a doctor who knows them)
- Simplicity (no confusing billing systems)
- Relationship (continuity, not rotations)
- Peace of mind
- Feeling known
- Not feeling rushed

**Lead with what patients buy, not how the model works.**

> **Wrong:** "Direct Primary Care is a membership-based model that eliminates
> third-party payer relationships."
>
> **Right:** "More time with your doctor, easier access, and a doctor who
> actually knows you."

---

## Language Rules: Speak Like a Patient

The single most common mistake in DPC marketing is writing for colleagues
instead of patients. Language that sounds professional to a clinician is
often invisible to a patient.

### Translate every clinical/operational phrase before it goes to copy

| Instead of this (doctor-speak) | Write this (patient-speak) |
|---|---|
| "Comprehensive longitudinal primary care through a value-based membership model" | "More time with your doctor, easier access, and simple monthly pricing" |
| "We optimize utilization across the care continuum" | "When something comes up, you can reach us, and we have time for you" |
| "Direct Primary Care membership" (as an opener) | The benefit first, model second |
| "Appointment availability" | "When you need to be seen" |
| "Panel size limitations" | "A doctor who actually knows you" |
| "Transparent pricing structure" | "No surprise bills" |

**Rule:** Lead with what the patient gets, not how the model is structured.
The model explanation can follow once interest is established.

## The Five-Second Clarity Standard

Any homepage hero must communicate all five of the following within five
seconds, without scrolling:

1. **What do you do?** — Name the care type (primary care, GYN, concierge, etc.)
2. **Who do you help?** — Name the patient (families, adults in X city, women 35+, etc.)
3. **Why is this different?** — The one thing that makes this practice worth switching for
4. **What does it cost me?** — At minimum a signal ("starting at $X/mo," "simple monthly membership")
5. **What do I do next?** — The CTA. One action, visible without scrolling.

If the hero answers fewer than five, it's incomplete. Revise before shipping.

**The launch mindset:** A site doesn't need to be big. It needs to be clear,
trustworthy, and conversion-focused. Clarity ships; perfection doesn't.

## Homepage Section Architecture

The homepage is a path, not a pile of sections. This order follows patient
psychology: what is this → why does it matter → does it solve my problem →
how does it work → what does it cost → who is this doctor → what's stopping
me → what do I do.

| # | Section | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hero** | What you do, who it's for, the main benefit, the next step |
| 2 | **Problem** | Name the frustration patients already feel |
| 3 | **Solution** | Your model, in plain English |
| 4 | **Benefits** | Why each feature matters to *them* (not feature specs) |
| 5 | **How It Works** | A simple, low-friction path to join (3 steps max) |
| 6 | **Pricing** | Enough clarity that cost is never a mystery |
| 7 | **About** | A human reason to trust the clinician |
| 8 | **FAQ** | Answer objections before they stop action |
| 9 | **Final CTA** | Tell them exactly what to do next |

Do not shuffle this order without a documented reason. It exists because it
mirrors how patients actually think.

## Section-by-Section Copy Rules

### Hero Section

**Win or lose them in five seconds.**

The hero must contain:
- A specific, clear headline (what you do + who it's for + main benefit)
- One subhead or supporting sentence (membership type + patient description)
- One CTA button (the single primary action)

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Healthcare reimagined." | "Primary care built around time, access, and a real relationship with your doctor." |
| "Learn More" | "Schedule a Meet & Greet" |
| No subhead | "Membership-based care for people who want longer visits and a doctor who knows them." |

Weak hero copy sounds nice and says nothing. Strong hero copy is specific
enough that a patient can immediately decide whether it's for them.

---

### Problem Section

**Name the frustration they already feel.**

Patients aren't searching for a healthcare delivery model. They're searching
because their current care is broken in some way. The problem section names
what they're tired of. This builds emotional connection: "this practice gets
why I'm frustrated."

Proven patient frustrations to draw from (use those relevant to the specific
practice and audience):
- Rushed visits
- Long waits
- Hard-to-reach doctors
- Not feeling heard
- Surprise bills
- Confusing systems
- No continuity
- Feeling like a number
- Weeks for an appointment
- Having to repeat their whole health history every time

**Example problem section copy:**
> "Primary care shouldn't feel rushed, confusing, or impossible to access.
> If you're tired of waiting weeks, spending minutes with your doctor, or
> feeling like no one knows your health story, there's a better way."

Do not invent clinical language for patient frustrations. Use the words
patients actually use.

---

### Solution Section

**Explain the model in plain English.**

Once the problem is named, introduce the model as the answer. The goal is
that the patient thinks "that makes sense." This is not the place for
operational detail.

> Instead of billing insurance for every visit (rushed appointments, surprise
> costs), patients pay a clear membership fee that gives them more direct
> access, longer visits, and a stronger relationship with their doctor.

**Critical:** Don't make the model sound unusual or complicated. You're
helping a patient nod along, not passing a health economics exam.

---

### Benefits Section

**Features vs. benefits: the fundamental distinction.**

A feature is what the practice offers. A benefit is why the patient cares.
Never list features without translating them.

| Feature | Benefit (why the patient cares) |
|---|---|
| Longer appointments | More time to talk through what's really going on |
| Direct messaging | Easier access the moment a question comes up |
| Transparent pricing | Fewer surprises, more control over cost |
| Smaller patient panel | A doctor who actually knows you |
| Same-day access | You get seen when it matters, not weeks from now |
| No insurance billing | No EOBs, no surprise bills, no prior auth delays |

Every feature on every client's site must have its benefit translation applied
before it goes into copy.

---

### How It Works Section

**Make the model feel easy.**

When people don't understand the process, they hesitate. When it feels simple,
they act. Three steps is the right number. Do not use more.

Standard DPC/concierge three-step structure:
1. Schedule a meet & greet → Get to know the practice, ask questions, see if it's the right fit
2. Choose your membership → Simple, transparent pricing that fits your needs
3. Start receiving care → More time, easier access, a more personal experience

Adjust step language for the specific practice. Never use more than three steps.

---

### Pricing Section

**Don't hide the price.**

Hiding pricing does more damage than showing it. When cost is unclear, most
patients assume it's too expensive.

When price is hidden, patients think:
- "Can I even afford this?"
- "Is this $50 a month or $500?"
- "Do I have to call just to find out?"
- "Is this only for wealthy people?"

Every unanswered pricing question is a reason not to reach out.

**Minimum acceptable pricing transparency for any DPC/concierge site:**
- Starting price per month
- Whether family plans are available
- That there are no surprise visit fees

Exact price is ideal. "Starting at $X/month" is acceptable. No price is a
conversion killer.

---

### About Section

**Build trust, not a CV.**

Credentials matter but they don't create emotional trust. The about section's
job is to give a human reason to trust this specific clinician.

Patients want to know:
- Who are you, really?
- Why did you build this practice?
- What kind of care do you believe in?
- What will it feel like to be your patient?

A list of board certifications and fellowship training answers none of those.
A mission paragraph does.

**Example:**
> "After years in traditional healthcare, [Dr. Name] wanted a practice where
> visits weren't rushed, patients felt heard, and care could be made with time
> and thought. That's why she built a membership-based practice focused on
> access, relationship, and personalized care."

Write the mission. The credentials can follow in a secondary bio block.

---

### FAQ Section

**Answer objections before they stop action.**

A strong FAQ moves patients past hesitation by addressing what they're already
thinking. Common high-priority questions for DPC/concierge/membership practices:

- Do you accept insurance?
- Is this health insurance?
- What's included in the membership?
- Can I still use insurance for labs & imaging?
- What happens if I need urgent care?
- Do you see children?
- Do you offer virtual visits?
- Can I cancel my membership?
- What if I already have a PCP?
- Is this a fit if I have chronic conditions?

Do not use the FAQ section to bury important information. It is a trust-building
tool, not a footnotes section.

---

### CTA Rules

**One primary action. Visible everywhere. Repeated consistently.**

CTA placement: header nav, hero section, after benefits, near pricing, at
page bottom. Every CTA on a page must be the same action.

Primary CTA options by practice stage:
- **Pre-launch or building waitlist:** "Join the Waitlist"
- **Open, educating prospects:** "Schedule a Meet & Greet"
- **Ready to enroll:** "Become a Member" or "Join Today"
- **Inquiry-first:** "Request More Information"

Never use:
- "Learn More" (tells patients nothing about what happens next)
- "Click Here"
- Two different CTAs competing on the same page

## The DPC-Specific Dual Challenge

A DPC or concierge practice website does two jobs at once that most sites
don't have to do:

1. **Build trust in the clinician** (as every healthcare site must)
2. **Make an unfamiliar model feel simple and worth it**

This creates a sequencing problem. Most visitors don't know what DPC is.
If the site leads with model explanation ("Direct Primary Care is a
membership-based alternative to fee-for-service primary care..."), it reads
like a textbook and loses the patient before they're hooked.

**The solution is always:** Lead with the benefit, then explain the structure.

> **Wrong lead:** "Direct Primary Care is a membership-based model where..."
> **Right lead:** "More time, easier access, and a doctor who knows you."

Benefit first. Model second. Always.

## Homepage vs. Landing Page Distinction

These are different tools for different jobs. Don't conflate them.

| Full website / homepage | Landing page |
|---|---|
| Your main digital home | Built for one focused action |
| Multiple sections, several possible next steps | Single audience, single message |
| Built to educate and orient any visitor | One clear CTA, no detours |
| Several paths through the content | Built to convert, not to explain everything |

**Use landing pages for:** employer outreach, waitlist sign-ups, open house
registrations, speaking events, community events, local campaigns.

A landing page should never have a navigation menu. It should never offer
multiple CTAs. It exists for one conversion.

---

## Site Structure: Minimum Viable Launch

Do not over-build before launch. A clear five-page site beats a bloated
twenty-page one.

**Must-have pages at launch:**
- Home (overview, model, benefits, pricing preview, CTA)
- About (who the clinician is, the care patients can expect)
- Membership/Pricing (what's included, what it costs, how to enroll)
- How It Works (the patient journey, simply)
- FAQ (common questions and objections)
- Contact/Join (a clear way to schedule or inquire)

**Add later:**
Blog, conditions treated, employer page, patient resources, testimonials,
local SEO pages, events, specialty services.

---

## Visual Trust Rules

Patients judge the feel of a site before they read a word. Image choices
either build or destroy the credibility the copy is working to create.

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Random stethoscope photos | Real photos of the clinician |
| Fake handshake shots | Real photos of the space |
| Empty exam rooms | Warm, patient-centered moments |
| Generic hospital imagery | Local community visuals |
| Models who don't look like your patients | People who match the ideal patient |

Generic stock photography signals "this is a template, not a real practice."
Authentic imagery signals "there is a real human behind this."

## Mobile-First Checklist

Most patients first encounter the practice on a phone. Every page must be
reviewed on a mobile device before launch.

On mobile, verify:
- Clear headline readable on a small screen
- CTA button large enough to tap without precision
- Fast load speed (no unoptimized images)
- Short, scannable sections (no walls of text)
- Readable font size (minimum 16px body)
- Simple hamburger menu (not a sprawling nav)
- Click-to-call phone number
- Forms that are easy to complete on mobile
- No awkward image cropping

---

## Conversion Essentials Checklist

Before any page is considered complete, verify:

- [ ] Clear headline above the fold
- [ ] Obvious, single call to action
- [ ] Simple navigation
- [ ] Mobile-friendly layout
- [ ] Fast load speed
- [ ] Easy-to-use contact form
- [ ] Pricing easy to find (not hidden)
- [ ] Strong FAQ section covering the top 8–10 objections
- [ ] Trust-building images (real clinician, real space)
- [ ] Local relevance (city, region, community)
- [ ] CTA repeated at logical intervals — no dead ends
- [ ] Model explanation in plain language

## SEO & Discoverability Rules

Healthcare is local. Search behavior is changing (traditional search AND AI
answer engines). Write for humans first; structure for machines second.

**Local relevance requirements:**
- Name the city and region served explicitly in body copy
- Reference nearby communities where relevant
- Include local photography when possible
- State the service area clearly

**SEO basics:**
- Helpful, clear content is the primary signal — not keyword density
- Local keywords embedded naturally in the copy
- A complete, optimized Google Business Profile
- Fast, mobile-first site

**AI answer engine readiness:** Patients increasingly ask AI tools to compare
care options. A site must clearly answer:
- What is direct primary care?
- How much does membership cost?
- Can I use insurance with DPC?
- Is this good for families?
- How do I join? What areas do you serve?

These questions should be answerable by any AI crawling the site. This means
they must be answered directly and plainly on the site itself, not buried
in PDFs or assumed knowledge.

## Mistakes That Kill Conversions

### Messaging mistakes
- Too much jargon (clinical or operational)
- Overexplaining DPC instead of selling the benefit
- Making it about the clinician's credentials instead of the patient's experience
- Vague benefits ("better care," "personalized attention")
- Hiding pricing
- Long paragraphs nobody reads
- Trying too hard to sound clever (clever after clear, never instead of)
- Not saying who the practice is for
- Not saying what the patient should do next

### Design mistakes
- Too many colors or fonts
- Poor spacing and weak contrast
- Low-quality or fake stock images
- Hard-to-read text
- Cluttered sections
- Inconsistent branding across pages
- CTA buttons that don't visually stand out
- Mobile layout problems

**The one rule:** Be clear before you're clever. A beautiful sentence that
doesn't help the patient understand is not useful — it's decoration.

---

## Pre-Launch Verification (priority order)

Before any client site or page goes live:

1. Explains what you do in 5 seconds
2. Main CTA visible without scrolling
3. Membership model explained simply (patient language, not clinical language)
4. Pricing is easy to find
5. Works well on mobile (tested on an actual phone)
6. Images are professional and trust-building
7. About section is human and credible (mission > CV)
8. Every page has a clear next step
9. Forms are easy to complete
10. The site feels like the care being delivered

**Priority waterfall for development:**
1. Clarity → 2. Trust → 3. Conversion → 4. SEO → 5. Expansion

Start with clarity. A simple site that converts beats a complex one that confuses.

## Quick-Reference: Weak vs. Strong Copy

| Context | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage headline | "Welcome to our innovative, patient-centered healthcare solutions through a modern membership model." | "Primary care with more time, easier access, and a doctor who knows you. Longer visits, simple pricing." |
| Feature → benefit | "Members receive extended visits and same-day appointments when available." | "You get more time to talk things through, and easier access when questions come up." |
| CTA | "Learn More" | "Schedule a Meet & Greet" / "Join the Waitlist" |
| Hero subhead | "Comprehensive longitudinal primary care through a value-based membership model." | "Membership-based care for people who want longer visits and a doctor who knows them." |
| Model description | "We optimize utilization across the care continuum." | "When something comes up, you can reach us, and we have time for you." |

## How to Apply This Framework

**For website copy:** Run every section through the five silent patient
questions. Check that each section is doing its job in the section architecture.
Translate every feature to a benefit before the copy leaves this desk.

**For social captions:** Lead with a patient problem, frustration, or desire.
Not with a feature. Not with a model explanation. The social post is not an
education lecture — it is a trust touchpoint.

**For email subject lines and body copy:** Apply the patient language rules.
No clinical jargon. No model-first framing. What does the patient get?

**For proposals and landing pages:** Single audience, single message, single
CTA. Use the problem → solution → benefit → CTA structure.

**For any headline:** Apply the five-second clarity standard. Can a patient
who knows nothing about DPC read this headline and understand what they'd get?

This framework is not a template. It is a decision filter. Every copy decision
runs through it.
